Raising capital, by asset class.

These guides set out how capital is raised in four asset classes, what institutional allocators look at in each, and how we run a mandate.

Download the brochuresBook a Consultation

Four asset classes

What you need to know

Each asset class is raised differently. The preparation starts with knowing how.

  1. 01

    Private Equity

    Closed-ended funds and single transactions that buy stakes in private companies, raised in closes from institutions and private wealth.

    Read the guideBrochure (PDF)

  2. 02

    Private Real Estate

    Funds, joint ventures and single-asset vehicles that buy, develop or reposition property, raised against a defined strategy or a named pipeline.

    Read the guideBrochure (PDF)

  3. 03

    Private Infrastructure

    Funds and vehicles that own energy, transport, utility, digital and social assets, raised from investors with long holding periods.

    Read the guideBrochure (PDF)

  4. 04

    Hedge Funds

    Open-ended funds and managed accounts that trade liquid markets, raised continuously and often starting with a founding investor.

    Read the guideBrochure (PDF)

Who does the capital serve?

Allocators invest on behalf of others. A pension fund invests for its members.

An endowment invests for the students and research it funds, and a sovereign fund for the citizens of its state. Every raise is read by people who answer for someone else’s capital, and we prepare each one with that reader in mind.
