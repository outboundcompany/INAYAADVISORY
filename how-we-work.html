How a raise runs

Five stages, from the discovery conversation to the close.

Revised November 2026

Inaya Advisory is an independent capital raising firm for private markets. We do one thing: put your raise in front of the investors most likely to commit, through our own network and through research-led approaches to investors we have not yet met, and keep those conversations moving.

What you pay for is our time and our network, working on your raise from the first week. Not a review of your documents, and not a promise of capital.

Stage 1 · Before the engagement letter

Discovery and senior review

Abbas Chothia, Client Advisor · David Hunter, Senior Advisor

A discovery conversation with Abbas on what you are raising, your timeline and what has already been tried. David then reads your materials the way a pension investment committee would and sets out his view in writing. You get our proposed approach, or a clear answer, with reasons, if your raise is not a fit. We do not take a mandate we cannot serve.

Stage 2 · Weeks 1 and 2

Build

A written brief on your raise, your record and who has backed you. An investor map built for your raise, starting from the investors most like the ones who already backed you, with the decision maker at each institution. You approve every name before anyone is contacted.

Stage 3 · From week 3

Introductions and approaches

David Hunter presents your mandate personally to the pension funds, endowments and institutions he knows, and to the consultants who advise them. Beyond his network, we approach investors who are committing to raises like yours now, in planned groups. Every message is reviewed by our broker-dealer before it is sent, and kept on record.

Stage 4 · Every week

Reporting

A written update: every investor contacted, their response and the next step, by stage, and what we changed because of what investors said. One record of every investor conversation, which stays yours when the mandate ends.

Stage 5 · Through to the close

We stay in the raise

After the introduction, we prepare you for each meeting, follow up with every investor until there is an answer, track what each investor asks for in diligence, and keep working the rest of the list. David joins the conversations that need him, by video as standard. If you want him at a meeting or conference in person, travel is agreed and paid by you in advance.

We act for you only and take no compensation from investors.

The full working detail of each stage, week by week, is shared with every firm considering an engagement with Inaya Advisory.

What to expect

Raising capital is closer to a series of tests than a formula. Here is what we can and cannot promise.

  • No one can guarantee capital. Not us, and not any advisor. Every investor decides through its own approval process: an investment committee, often a consultant, sometimes a board. Those take months, and we do not control them.
  • What you get is access and a process. Access to our network and to the investors our research finds, and a process run properly from the first approach onward.
  • Start early. Institutional commitments take months. The best time to begin is six to nine months before you need the capital.
  • The first approaches are a test. The first group of investors tells us how your raise is read. We adjust before the next one.
  • No black box. Every approach is reviewed by our broker-dealer before it goes out and kept on record, so you, the investor and the regulator can see exactly what was sent.

What we raise for

Equity only. We do not raise for debt, private credit or lending strategies.

  • First-time funds. Where the record comes from deals the team did elsewhere, attributed person by person.
  • Successor funds. Positioned against the last fund's results.
  • Co-investment and deal-by-deal vehicles. Equity for a single transaction, alongside a fund or on its own.
  • Evergreen and open-ended equity funds. Where the valuation policy and liquidity terms are what gets diligenced.
  • Private companies. Growth equity and late-stage rounds with a clear use of proceeds.

What we raise for

Equity for fund commitments, co-investment and deal-by-deal vehicles, and private companies. We do not raise for debt, private credit or lending strategies.

Questions before you appoint us

Why is there an engagement fee?

It pays for our time: presenting your mandate to our network in the strongest possible way, and running live approaches to investors, shaped by current market conditions. It is credited in full against the success fee.

Is the engagement fee refundable?

No. It pays for work we do whatever investors decide. That is why David reviews your raise and we tell you whether it fits before the engagement letter is signed.

Do you charge on investors we already know?

No. The success fee applies only to investors we introduce, named on a list you approve. Your existing investors and re-ups are yours.

Can you guarantee we will raise?

No. No advisor can, and securities rules bar any promise of it. Investors decide after their own diligence. What we commit to is the process on this page, and a weekly record of every investor we contact.

How do you find investors?

Two ways at once. David Hunter presents your mandate personally to the US institutions he has worked with over forty years: pension funds, endowments and the consultants who advise them. Alongside that, we screen public records to find the family offices, RIAs and institutions committing to raises like yours now, in the US and, where your offering documents and local rules allow, in other countries.

Do we have to work with you exclusively?

No. Our mandates are non-exclusive. You can work with other advisers at the same time, and we are paid only on the investors we introduce.

How many mandates do you take at once?

It depends on the mandates, and there is no fixed number. When we reach capacity, we pause new mandates and say so on this page.

Have you closed a raise like ours?

Not yet. Inaya was established in June 2026 and has been fully operational since mid-July. We have two mandates in progress. Both are under NDA, so we cannot name them.

Why do raises stall?

More often on operational diligence than on the strategy: a valuation policy that cannot be explained, missing policies, or a record that does not survive a reference call. A first close that never arrives also stalls a raise, because investors read it as a signal.

What should be ready before the first approach?

A two-page teaser with the headline numbers, your deck, your track record with every number traceable to source, a completed due diligence questionnaire, and a data room with your governing documents, financials and policies. David's senior review tells you what is missing.
