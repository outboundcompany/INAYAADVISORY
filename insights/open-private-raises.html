Open private raises

Of 951 private offerings of US$50 million or more still open, most report little or nothing sold.

Book a Consultation

Form D analysis

What you need to know

  1. 01

    Most report nothing sold

    Six in ten of the 951 open offerings of US$50 million or more have reported nothing sold.[1]

  2. 02

    Most of the money is unraised

    US$129.8 billion of the US$149.3 billion sought is still to be raised.[2]

  3. 03

    Time alone does not close a raise

How much has been sold?

Most of these raises have made little progress on paper. 567 of the 951 reported nothing sold on their latest filing. A further 253 had sold less than a quarter of their target. Only 46 had sold more than half.

How much is still to be raised?

Together the 951 offerings sought US$149.3 billion. On their most recent filings, US$129.8 billion of that is still to be raised.

How long have raises been open?

Time alone does not close a raise. 470 of these offerings have been open for six months or longer, and 414 of those have sold less than a quarter of their target.

Which industries lead?

The pattern is broad. Among the 951, real estate accounts for 440 offerings and investing for 233. Technology and health care together account for 69.

What do the raises that close have in place?

The filings do not say why any single raise has not closed.

In our experience, the raises that do close tend to have four things in place.

01

Materials that answer the committee

An investment committee comes to a raise with a familiar set of questions. Materials that answer them before the first meeting allow that meeting to be spent on the decision, not on the basics.

02

A considered sequence

The order in which allocators see a raise shapes how the market reads it. A raise shown to everyone at once reads differently from one introduced in a deliberate order, to the right allocators first.

03

An introduction from someone known

Allocators give their time to people they know. An introduction from someone the allocator already knows is read and answered in a way that an unsolicited approach rarely is.

04

Follow-through to terms

Interest is not a commitment. Diligence requests, follow-up questions and the commitment conversations need to be carried through, without gaps, until terms are agreed.
