The FCA proposes to exempt performance fees from the workplace pensions charge cap.

November 12, 20263 min read

In the Market

November 12, 2026

3 min read

LinkedInEmail

The Financial Conduct Authority opened a consultation, CP26/40, on exempting certain performance fees from the charge cap on default funds in the workplace pensions it regulates. The cap is 0.75% of funds under management and was introduced in 2015.

The FCA said the proposals would make it easier for pension schemes to invest in asset classes where performance fees are common, such as private equity and venture capital. They largely mirror the exemption the Department for Work and Pensions made for trust-based schemes in 2023, with added safeguards on transparency, the allocation of fees between savers and the valuation of private assets. Providers could choose whether to use the exemption.

For a raiseWhat it means for a raise

This is a consultation. Responses are due by 14 January 2027, and the FCA expects to make final rules in summer 2027.

If adopted, the change would remove one obstacle between UK defined contribution savings and private equity funds. The FCA has said that any higher charge must be justified by additional value, so a manager approaching this channel should be ready to show how its fees relate to what savers receive.
