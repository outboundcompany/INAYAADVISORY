Investment Committee Review

We make your mandate defensible. Before any investor sees a raise, our senior team reviews it the way an investment committee will, finds what an allocator will challenge and fixes it, so the raise holds up in diligence.

Who reviews it

The review is led by our senior team, including a former chief investment officer of two US public pension systems.

01

A chief investment officer's reading

David Hunter, our Senior Advisor, was Executive Director and Chief Investment Officer of the North Dakota Retirement and Investment Office and of the US$40 billion Texas Municipal Retirement System. He reads each raise as the investment staff he led would have read it.

02

What allocators are asking now

Shayan Mohammed, our Managing Director, leads our investor coverage across North America, Europe and the Gulf. He brings the questions allocators are putting to managers in current conversations.

03

Before the first meeting

The review is done before outreach begins. A point settled before an allocator reads the raise does not have to be answered in diligence, where it costs the most time and confidence.

David Hunter's roles are from his earlier career and are not transactions of Inaya Advisory.

How the review runs

We read the raise as an allocator's committee will and set out in writing each point it is likely to challenge, then we change the raise until each point has an answer.

The committee's reading

We work through the deck, the track record, the terms and the data room in the order an allocator's staff does when they prepare a fund for their committee.

Each challenge in writing

Every point a committee is likely to raise is written down, with the reason it will be raised and how much it is likely to weigh on the decision.

Changed before outreach

We agree the changes with the manager and make them with the team. We then read the raise again, as a committee would, before it goes to any investor.

What we test

Six areas decide most committee reviews. We test each one the way an allocator's diligence will.

01

Track record presentation

Whether realised and unrealised results are shown apart, whether attribution holds at deal level, and whether every figure reconciles with the data room.

02

Terms

How the hurdle, the general partner commitment, key person provisions and governance compare with what allocators accept for the strategy and the fund size.

03

Fees

Whether the management fee and carried interest are supported by the strategy, the team and the fund size, and where a committee is likely to ask for a change.

04

The team

Who made the results, whether they are still at the firm, how investment decisions are taken and what happens if a key person leaves.

05

The pipeline

Whether the opportunities shown support the fund size and the pace of deployment, and whether they are specific enough for an allocator to check.

06

Data room gaps

What an allocator will ask for and not find, from deal-level data to policies and service provider details, closed before the first diligence call.

When managers come to us

A review is asked for when a raise has to hold up in front of a committee the first time it is read.

01

If you are about to launch a raise, come to us.

We review it before the first investor meeting, while every change can still be made without cost to a relationship.

02

If diligence stops at the same point, come to us.

We identify the point allocators are stopping on and the reason for it, and we change what they are shown before the next diligence process.

03

If an allocator has passed and the reason is unclear, come to us.

We read the raise as that committee did, tell the manager what most likely decided it and what to change before the next one.

What the manager receives

At the end of the review the manager holds a written assessment and a raise that has been changed to answer it.

The written review

Each point a committee will challenge, ranked by how much it is likely to weigh, with the evidence an allocator will look for when it does.

The changes, made

We work with the team on the track record, the terms, the pipeline and the data room until each point has an answer a committee can check.

A committee rehearsal

Our senior team puts the committee's questions to the manager before any allocator does, and the answers are tested against the materials.

How it differs from Positioning & Materials

Positioning & Materials builds the deck and the documents. Investment Committee Review is the judgment on whether the raise will survive a committee, and on what has to change for it to do so. A manager whose materials were prepared elsewhere can engage us for the review alone.

Positioning & Materials
Before a mandate or on its own

The review can open a capital raising mandate, or stand on its own for a manager that runs its own raise and wants an allocator's reading first.

Capital raising mandates
Held in confidence

What we find in a review stays between us and the manager. Nothing from it is shared with an investor without the manager's agreement.
