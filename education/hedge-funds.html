Hedge Funds

Hedge funds raise capital continuously into open-ended funds that trade liquid markets.

Download the brochureBook a Consultation

Introduction to hedge funds

What you need to know

  1. 01

    Structure

    Open-ended fund, or a separately managed account.

  2. 02

    Liquidity

    Subscriptions and redemptions at set dates, with notice periods.

  3. 03

    Investors

    Pensions, endowments, funds of funds, family offices, private wealth.

  4. 04

    Timing

    Continuous, often starting with a founding investor.

How is a hedge fund raised?

A hedge fund raise has no final close. Investors can redeem.

Most hedge funds are open-ended. The manager raises for as long as the strategy has capacity.

Open-ended, with no final close

  1. Subscribe

    Investors subscribe at set dates, often monthly or quarterly.

  2. Invest

    In some funds an initial lock-up applies.

  3. Redeem

    Investors redeem at set dates, subject to notice periods.

How does a new manager raise?

A new manager often raises in stages.

Raising in stages

  1. Founding investor

    May commit early in return for better terms.

  2. Founders' share class

    Can be offered to the first investors.

  3. Institutional allocators

    Many wait for a track record and a minimum fund size before they invest.

Who invests in hedge funds?

Investors include pension funds, endowments, foundations, sovereign wealth funds, funds of hedge funds, family offices and private wealth platforms. Some invest through separately managed accounts held in their own name.

  • Pension funds
  • Endowments
  • Foundations
  • Sovereign wealth funds
  • Funds of hedge funds
  • Family offices
  • Private wealth platforms

Who does the capital serve?

Many hedge fund allocators are pension funds. They invest on behalf of teachers, nurses and other public employees.

Endowments, foundations, sovereign funds and family offices invest alongside them, each on behalf of the people and institutions it serves.

What do institutional allocators look at?

Allocators decide on evidence. We help you present it.

01

Return record

The monthly return history, how returns behaved in difficult markets, the size and length of losses from peak, and how much of the result came from the strategy itself.

02

Strategy and capacity

What the strategy does, why it has produced results, and how much capital it can take before results are affected.

03

Risk management

How positions are sized and limited, how risk is measured and reported, and who has the authority to reduce a position.

04

Operations

The administrator, the auditor, the prime broker and the valuation policy, and whether the fund is run to the operational standard an institution requires.
