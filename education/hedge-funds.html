How is hedge fund capital raised?

A hedge fund raise never closes; it runs continuously, and every monthly return becomes part of the pitch.

Hedge fund managers do not raise a fund and close it. They open a vehicle, accept subscriptions at each dealing date and keep raising for as long as capacity allows. The track record is published monthly, so the conversation with an allocator changes every time a new net asset value is struck. A manager is judged on the path of returns, not on a projected outcome.

Strategy sets the audience. Equity long/short, global macro, credit, relative value, multi-strategy and systematic quant books each sit in a different slot of an allocator's portfolio, with a different expected correlation to equities and bonds. Most committees first ask what role the fund plays in their book, a diversifier, a return enhancer or a liquid credit sleeve, before they ask about returns.

The continuous raise, from seed to scale

  1. Seed and launch

    Founder and seed capital fund the master; administrator, auditor and prime broker are appointed before trading.

  2. Build the record

    Monthly returns accumulate; most institutions want a multi-year record through at least one stressed market.

  3. Pass ODD

    Allocators review service providers, valuation, controls and cyber, often with an on-site visit.

  4. Scale and hold

    Pensions, sovereigns and consultants allocate; tickets grow as the record and assets lengthen.

  5. Manage capacity

    The manager soft closes, then hard closes, to protect returns and keep liquidity terms honest.

What allocators measure in a hedge fund

Drawdown profile

Maximum peak to trough loss, how long it lasted and how many months the fund took to recover to its prior high.

What allocators look for

Losses consistent with stated risk, quick recovery, and no single drawdown that breaks the strategy's own thesis.

Volatility

Annualised standard deviation of monthly returns, read against the stated target and the strategy peer group.

What allocators look for

Volatility kept inside the target band, without sharp regime changes that suggest style drift or rising gross exposure.

Sharpe and Sortino

Excess return per unit of total volatility, and per unit of downside volatility only.

What allocators look for

Ratios that hold across sub-periods and are not produced by smoothed marks or a single good year.

Beta and correlation

Sensitivity and correlation of returns to equity and bond indices, and to the allocator's existing managers.

What allocators look for

Low or explained market exposure, and correlation that does not rise sharply when equities fall.

What diligence covers

01

Administrator independence

Allocators expect a recognized third-party administrator to strike the net asset value, hold the shareholder register and process subscriptions and redemptions. They check that cash moves only with dual authorisation, that the administrator prices positions independently where possible, and that investors can receive transparency reports directly from it.

02

Valuation policy

Committees read the written valuation policy and ask who prices hard-to-value positions, how broker quotes are sourced and how often marks are challenged. A valuation committee with independent members, documented overrides and reconciliation to the administrator carries weight, especially for credit, distressed and side pocket assets.

03

Prime brokers and counterparties

Allocators review which prime brokers, swap counterparties and custodians hold assets, how margin is negotiated and whether the fund can move positions quickly if one counterparty weakens. More than one prime broker, signed ISDA terms and daily collateral monitoring are expected once a fund reaches institutional size.
